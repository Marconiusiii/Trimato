Trimato Help

Save and export

Save the project

  • Choose File > Save or press Command-S from any pane or tool window to save the open project.
  • Choose File > Save As or press Shift-Command-S to save another project package.
  • Choose File > Close Project or press Shift-Command-W to close the project.

Closing the project, opening another project, or quitting Trimato asks you to Save, Don’t Save, or Cancel when changes remain since your last Save. Don’t Save restores the last explicitly saved version, including when macOS has autosaved newer edits. Cancel or a failed save keeps the project open.

Mute tracks before export

Choose an audio track in Timeline, then turn on Mute Track. Repeat for each track you want silent. The setting affects the whole track in preview and exported audio, including audio transitions, not just the clip beneath the playhead. It does not remove clips, change timing, or hide video. Mute is saved with the project and can be undone. Turn Mute Track off to restore that track’s sound.

Choose the export range

  • Set both In and Out points in the project Editor to export that range.
  • Clear both In and Out points to export the complete project.
  • Place In earlier than Out.

Understand the exported project frame

An audio-only project has no picture and offers audio output formats. When a project contains video, it also offers video output formats and uses the project's resolution and frame rate.

Every video export uses the project's resolution and frame rate. Mixed source dimensions are proportionally fitted without stretching or automatic cropping. Narrower sources are pillarboxed, wider sources are letterboxed, and unused parts of the project frame are black.

An audio-only timeline section inside a video project uses black picture for that section unless a video cutaway supplies the picture.

The exported file uses the project frame rate. When a clip uses a different frame rate, its speed and audio length stay the same.

Preserve HDR or export SDR video

  1. Open Trimato > Settings > Video.
  2. Leave Preserve HDR on to retain HDR brightness and color from supported recordings. It is on by default.
  3. Export as HEVC MP4, HEVC movie, ProRes 422 LT, ProRes 422, or ProRes 422 HQ.

HDR output supports video filters, transitions, and burned-in captions. HEVC HDR exports use 10-bit video with Dolby Vision metadata; ProRes exports retain HLG HDR.

Turn Preserve HDR off to convert HDR video to standard dynamic range, or SDR. Use this when you need H.264 output. HDR project exports offer compatible formats; an incompatible HDR format request fails instead of silently converting the picture. The standalone Original format option passes through supported media and does not apply SDR conversion.

Open File > Project Settings to match the intended resolution and frame rate. A 4K recording placed in a 1080p project exports at 1080p. A 120 fps recording placed in a 30 fps project exports at 30 fps. Variable-frame-rate sources use the project's fixed frame grid rather than their exact original frame timing.

Preserving HDR does not make video encoding lossless. The project format, filters, and export codec still affect the finished picture.

Choose audio quality

For mono or stereo audio-only work or a mixed video soundtrack, choose the output format to suit the next editing or delivery step. Spatial QuickTime exports keep the source audio encoding for unchanged audio or use uncompressed audio for supported volume and transition edits.

Audio export choices
FormatWhen to choose it
M4A Apple Lossless or FLACKeep the rendered audio without lossy compression.
WAV audio, 24-bitSave uncompressed audio for further editing.
WAV audio, 16-bitUse when delivery requires 16-bit uncompressed audio.
ProRes 422 LT, 422, or 422 HQ movieKeep uncompressed 24-bit audio inside the video file.
M4A AAC audio, H.264, or HEVC videoUse compressed AAC audio for delivery. This re-encodes audio and is not lossless.

Clip processing retains the source audio sample rate. A project mixes at the highest sample rate among its source audio, converting lower-rate sources to that common rate. Mixed project exports are stereo; standalone mono audio can remain mono. Spatial exports retain the spatial channels and a stereo playback alternative. Unsupported multichannel sources without a usable mono or stereo track cannot be mixed.

A lossless format avoids an additional lossy encoding stage. It does not recover information missing from the original recording or undo changes made with filters, volume, or other mix controls.

Preserve iPhone Spatial Audio

Import your iPhone recordings, trim and arrange them on the timeline, and add volume changes, fades, Cross Fade, or Fade Out/In. Compatible recordings keep their spatial sound through the finished edit.

  1. Choose Export Project, or Export Clip for a standalone edit.
  2. Choose Audio: Preserve Spatial Audio or High-quality Stereo.
  3. Choose a compatible Format. Preserve Spatial Audio uses QuickTime movies. For stereo, 24-bit WAV, Apple Lossless, FLAC, and ProRes offer uncompressed or lossless audio; AAC formats use lossy compression.
  4. Export the complete edit, or set In and Out beforehand to export a selected range.

Trimato remembers your audio choice. Combining spatial recordings or changing their audio creates larger files with uncompressed audio and a stereo playback alternative. Ordered cuts from one recording with unchanged audio keep the original encoding. Processed spatial exports do not include the original Apple Audio Mix analysis information.

Spatial preservation supports compatible recording layouts and sample rates, including standard iPhone and Cinematic recordings together. Other audio effects, voice adjustments, pan, balance, width, channel routing, ducking, and mixing spatial recordings with ordinary audio currently use a labeled stereo preview. Export explains when High-quality Stereo is required.

Wait for preview preparation after changing the edit. In Settings > Video, keep Preserve HDR on for compatible HDR output; turn it off for SDR, including H.264 QuickTime.

Cinematic video can be edited as picture, but converted exports do not retain editable focus and depth information. Select the clip and press Command-I to check its actual dimensions and frame rate; Cinematic mode can use different recording settings.

Understand which media export uses

Playback proxies are used for responsive preview and editing, not as the final-quality sources for a project export. Trimato returns to the original source files and applies the source ranges, timeline arrangement, cutaways, audio choices, project resolution, and project frame rate saved in the project.

If macOS cannot use an original directly, Trimato makes a temporary full-quality copy for export. Trimato removes that copy when export finishes, is cancelled, or fails.

Keep every original source used by the timeline available. A cached proxy does not make an offline original available for final export.

Export a project

  1. If the project contains caption drafts, choose Timeline > Captions > Finalize Captions and resolve any passages that remain drafts.
  2. Choose File > Export Project or press Command-E.
  3. Enter a filename and choose a location and format.
  4. If the project has captions, choose Burn Captions into Video, WebVTT Sidecar File, SRT Sidecar File, or No Captions from Captions.
  5. Choose Export. A progress bar will appear. Choose Cancel Export if you want to stop.

Burn Captions into Video places the captions in the exported video. WebVTT Sidecar File and SRT Sidecar File save a caption file beside the exported media with the same filename. No Captions exports the project without captions. Audio formats offer WebVTT Sidecar File, SRT Sidecar File, and No Captions.

Export the caption track

  1. Choose Timeline > Captions > Finalize Captions. Resolve any passages that remain drafts.
  2. Choose Timeline > Captions > Export Captions.
  3. Choose WebVTT, SRT, or Plain Text.
  4. Enter a filename and choose Export.

Caption files cannot be exported while drafts remain. If the project has In and Out points, the exported caption file contains the cues in that range and begins at zero. Clear both points to export the complete caption track. Plain Text places the project title first, followed by each caption as a separate paragraph without timecodes.

Export a standalone clip

  1. Open the clip in a standalone Clip Editor.
  2. Set both In and Out points to export a range, or clear both to export the complete edit.
  3. Activate Export Clip or press Command-E.
  4. In the Export Clip dialog, enter a filename, choose a location and format, then activate Export.
  5. A progress bar will appear. Choose Cancel Export if you want to stop.

Check an export result

When export finishes, Trimato announces “Export complete.” If Trimato notifications are allowed, macOS sends a system notification containing the exported filename. Open the saved movie or audio file from its chosen location.

Review export notification permission

Choose Trimato > Settings and read Export Notifications. If the permission is Not requested, activate Allow Export Notifications. If the permission is Not allowed, open System Settings, choose Notifications, choose Trimato, and turn on Allow Notifications.
