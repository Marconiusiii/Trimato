Trimato Help

Build and edit the project timeline

The Track picker chooses which track appears in the native Timeline clips list. Clips and transitions appear once each in playback order. Primary tracks retain magnetic editing. Additional tracks can also contain independently positioned clips and gaps for music, effects, and layered material. Use Timeline clips to review and arrange the project, Clip Editor to change a source edit, Transition Editor to change transition timing, and Editor for project playback and direct playhead-based editing.

Choose and manage a track

Use the Track picker or press Command-Option-Up Arrow and Command-Option-Down Arrow to change tracks. When you use those shortcuts from Editor, Trimato announces the active track and its direct-edit clip without moving VoiceOver out of Editor. Track Actions adds, renames, moves, or deletes user-created video and audio tracks. Primary tracks cannot be deleted.

Place an edited clip

Append to Timeline
Adds the clip after the last timeline clip.
Insert and Split
Splits the clip beneath the project playhead, inserts the new clip between the two parts, and moves later clips forward. After insertion, the project playhead moves to the incoming clip’s end so consecutive inserts remain in the order you make them.
Insert and Overwrite
Keeps the portion before the project playhead, replaces the remainder of that clip, and keeps later clips after the inserted clip.
Insert on Top with Source Audio
Shows and plays the inserted clip over the project for its duration.
Insert on Top over Primary Audio
Shows the inserted clip while the primary timeline audio continues.

Blade at the playhead

  1. Move the project playhead inside a timeline clip.
  2. Activate Blade at playhead or press Command-B while focus is in the Editor.

The two resulting instances receive lettered names such as Clip01 A and Clip01 B. The playhead remains at the new edit point.

Edit the active track without leaving Editor

  1. Keep VoiceOver on any control in Editor and move the shared project playhead to the required time.
  2. Press Command-Option-Up Arrow or Command-Option-Down Arrow until Trimato announces the required track and clip.
  3. Press Command-[ to trim that clip's start to the project playhead, or press Command-] to trim its end to the project playhead.
  4. On an additional track, press plain [ to move the complete clip so its head begins at the playhead, or press plain ] to move it so its tail ends at the playhead. These positioning commands do not trim the stored source.

The direct-edit clip is remembered separately for each track. If none has been remembered, Trimato uses the incoming clip at an edit point, a clip containing the playhead, the next clip, or the last earlier clip. A successful trim announces the clip, track, trimmed edge, and time. The operation can be undone.

Arrange timeline clips

  1. Choose a track and focus a clip in Timeline Clips.
  2. Press Space to pick up that clip. Trimato announces Moving, the clip name, and its position.
  3. On primary tracks, press Left Arrow or Up Arrow to move it one clip earlier. Press Right Arrow or Down Arrow to move it one clip later. On additional tracks, those arrows nudge it one project frame earlier or later instead.
  4. Press Space again, or Escape, to drop the clip. The list is reordered only when you drop it. Command-Z undoes the complete move.

For a specific destination, keep the source selected for moving and focus the destination clip. Open Selected Element Actions with Control-Enter, or the context menu with VoiceOver’s VO-Shift-M command. Choose Move To…, then Start, Before, After, or End. Before and After use the focused destination; Start and End use the active track. Without a picked-up clip, Start and End move the focused clip directly. Focusing another clip does not change the clip selected for moving.

Press Enter or Return on a clip to open Clip Editor, matching a click or normal button activation. Space selects for moving only in Timeline Clips; Space still plays or pauses in Editor and Clip Editor. If VoiceOver Quick Nav is using the arrow keys, turn Quick Nav off for plain-arrow movement, or use the menu commands.

On absolute-position (additional video and audio) tracks, plain Left Arrow or Up Arrow nudges the focused clip one project frame earlier; Right Arrow or Down Arrow nudges it one frame later. You do not need to pick it up first. Each immediate nudge can be undone. If you have picked up a clip, the arrows adjust that clip’s proposed start time until you drop it. Trimato announces its start frame. A project without a defined frame rate uses 30 fps for nudges. Nudges leave neighboring clips in place and reject overlap or crossing another clip on the same track; touching edges are allowed. Linked audio moves with picture, and a collision on the linked track also blocks the nudge. Clips with attached transitions cannot be nudged until those transitions are removed.

The clip context menu keeps Move To… as the single ordering submenu; it no longer includes separate Move Earlier and Move Later items. The Timeline menu’s Move Clip Earlier and Move Clip Later commands remain available as an alternative to plain arrows. On primary tracks they reorder by one clip; on additional tracks they nudge by one frame. If a clip is picked up, they adjust that clip.

Current identifies the clip under the project playhead. Selected identifies only a clip you have picked up; moving VoiceOver focus does not select a clip or change the playhead. Ordinary clip navigation does not announce list positions. List positions are announced only when picking up and reordering a clip on a primary track; absolute-position nudges announce the start frame.

VoiceOver’s VO-Command-Shift-Space command holds the mouse button down. When that mouse-down is on a clip, use the arrow keys to choose its new position, then repeat the command to release the mouse button and drop the clip. An ordinary short click still opens Clip Editor. Pointer dragging and keyboard movement use the same pickup-and-drop session.

Press Command-C on a focused clip, focus a destination clip, then press Command-V to paste a copy after it. Command-Option-V moves the clip selected for moving, or the copied clip if no movement selection is active, after the destination. Video clips move among video tracks and audio clips move among audio tracks. Moving picture carries its linked audio. Moving audio directly makes it independent of its picture. Moves that would overlap linked material or invalidate a transition are rejected without changing the project. Copying does not copy transitions.

Trim a layered audio clip to the video ending

  1. In Editor, activate Go to End of Video or navigate to the required video edit point.
  2. Use Command-Option-Up Arrow or Command-Option-Down Arrow until Trimato announces the required audio track and long audio clip.
  3. Without leaving Editor, press Command-] to trim the announced clip's end to the shared project playhead.

The clip keeps its material before the shared project playhead. Later clips on that audio track move earlier by the removed duration. VoiceOver remains in Editor, and the operation can be undone. You can instead focus the clip in Timeline clips and choose Trim End to Project Playhead from its context menu.

Open a clip from Editor

Press C while focus is in Editor to open Clip Editor for the active-track clip at the playhead. At an edit point, Trimato opens the incoming clip. In a gap, it opens the next clip, but no clip is marked Current until the playhead is inside a clip. In Timeline Clips, Enter or Return opens the focused clip regardless of the playhead position.

Rename a timeline item

  1. Focus a timeline clip or cutaway.
  2. Choose Rename Clip from Selected Element Actions or the item's context menu.
  3. Enter a name that is not already used elsewhere in the timeline, then activate Rename.

Trimato automatically gives repeated source names stable letter suffixes such as A and B. A custom name applies only to that timeline instance, is saved with the project, and can be undone.

Remove a timeline item

  1. Focus a timeline clip or cutaway.
  2. Choose Delete from Timeline or press Delete.

Add and edit transitions

  1. Move the Editor playhead onto a clip, or focus the clip in Timeline clips.
  2. Press Command-T.
  3. For Fade, choose Fade In {clip name}, Fade Out {clip name}, or both. Both controls name the selected clip. Other transition types retain the Intro and Outro side controls.
  4. If the video clip has audio, choose Fade Audio or Crossfade Audio to apply the matching audio transition.
  5. Enter the duration in seconds, then activate Add.

The duration begins at 1.0 seconds and accepts fractional seconds such as 1.25. Trimato checks that the selected clip and any neighboring clip have enough media for the transition.

Use Quick Fade

  1. Move the Editor playhead onto a clip or to a shared cut between clips.
  2. Press F to open Quick Fade.
  3. Choose Fade In {clip name}, Fade Out {clip name}, or both. Fade Audio includes the matching linked audio for each selected fade.
  4. Enter the duration in seconds, then apply the fade.

At a shared cut from clip A to clip B, Fade In B applies at the beginning of B and Fade Out A applies at the end of A. Selecting both places both fades around that cut. Within a clip, at the start of the timeline, or at its final endpoint, both controls name the same clip. This behavior is the same on video and audio tracks.

Applying a fade uses a separate progress window. VoiceOver percentage announcements use low priority so navigation to Cancel can take precedence.

Use Quick Cross Dissolve or Quick Cross Fade

  1. Move the Editor playhead to an edit with an outgoing and incoming clip.
  2. Press X. A video track opens Quick Cross Dissolve. An audio track opens Quick Cross Fade.
  3. Confirm the displayed outgoing and incoming clip names.
  4. In a video context, choose Crossfade Audio when the clips’ audio should cross fade too.
  5. Enter the duration in seconds, then apply the transition.

Applying a transition uses a separate progress window with low-priority VoiceOver percentage announcements.

Transition types

Fade In
Gradually changes the selected video from black to the clip.
Fade Out
Gradually changes the selected video from the clip to black.
Audio Fade In
Gradually raises the clip audio from silence.
Audio Fade Out
Gradually lowers the clip audio to silence.
Cross Dissolve
Fades the incoming picture over the outgoing picture across their shared edit. Crossfade Audio blends both clips’ audio at the same time.
Cross Fade
Lowers the outgoing audio while raising the incoming audio across their shared edit.
Fade Out/Fade In
Fades outgoing video through black into incoming video, or fades outgoing audio through silence into incoming audio. Unlike a cross dissolve or cross fade, the two sources do not overlap.
Wipe Left
Replaces the outgoing picture with the incoming picture using an edge that moves left.
Wipe Right
Replaces the outgoing picture with the incoming picture using an edge that moves right.
Wipe Up
Replaces the outgoing picture with the incoming picture using an edge that moves upward.
Wipe Down
Replaces the outgoing picture with the incoming picture using an edge that moves downward.

Wipes affect video only. Audio-only projects offer audio fades and cross fades without video transition choices. Each transition appears as its own Timeline clips item. Activate it to edit or delete the transition without changing the source edit.

Trimato creates temporary render files so it can play transitions and adjusted audio. These files support the current preview and are normally removed when the preview changes or the project closes. Learn about temporary renders and cleanup.

Mute an audio track

Choose the audio track in the Track picker, then turn on Mute Track. Muting affects the whole track in preview and export, including audio transitions. It does not delete clips, change timing, or hide picture. The setting is saved with the project and can be undone. Turn it off to restore the track’s sound.

Adjust an audio clip

Activate a clip on an audio track to open Clip Editor. The Audio section provides gain, low, mid, and high equalization, high-pass and low-pass filters, and Reset Audio. Activate Update Clip to apply the settings.

Get information about a timeline item

VoiceOver focus on a clip or transition makes it the target for Command-I without picking up the clip. Press Command-I to open a separate Get Info window containing a fixed snapshot of that item’s details.

Set independent fade durations

Select Fade In {clip name} to show Fade In Duration, or Fade Out {clip name} to show Fade Out Duration. Selecting both exposes both fields. Values are in seconds and apply to the named clip and its corresponding linked audio fade. Clearing a checkbox hides its field without discarding the entered value. With neither fade selected, Apply Fade is unavailable. The same duration labels are used when editing an existing fade.

Additional video track fades change the clip's opacity, revealing the video underneath. Primary video fades reveal the black background when no picture is underneath.
