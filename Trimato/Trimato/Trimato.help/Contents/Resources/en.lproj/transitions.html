Add and edit transitions

Move the Editor playhead to a shared edit or focus an adjoining clip in Timeline Clips, then open Transitions. The tool pane holds the settings for that edit. Command-4 returns to the open tool.

Choose Cancel, press Escape, or choose the tool’s Close command in the File menu to close it. For an unfinished draft, choose whether to discard it or continue editing.

Successful application closes the pane and returns focus to the Editor or Timeline. If application fails, read the error window and choose OK to return to the transition settings. For insufficient source footage, shorten the duration or adjust the named clips’ trim points.

  1. Move the Editor playhead to a shared edit, or focus a clip with an adjoining clip in Timeline Clips.
  2. Press Command-T to open Transitions in the Tool pane.
  3. Choose the pair of clips from the Edit picker when more than one pair is available.
  4. Choose a Transition Type, then its options: Fade Style and Fade Color, Direction, or Action for Circle. For Wipes, Slide, Cover, Reveal, or Circle, choose an Ease setting. If both video clips have linked audio at the same edit, select Include Audio Transition, then choose Audio Transition Type and, for Crossfade, Audio Fade Curve.
  5. Enter the duration in seconds, then choose Add.

The duration begins at 1.0 seconds and accepts fractional seconds such as 1.25. The transition is centered on the shared edit and keeps the timeline duration unchanged. Each clip must contain at least half the transition duration. Overlapping video transitions and audio crossfades also need unused source footage equal to half the duration after the outgoing clip’s trim and before the incoming clip’s trim.

Prepare clips for a transition

Generators supply their picture or silence throughout a transition. Each timeline clip needs enough selected time for its half of the transition.

When a clip is too short for a transition, open the named clip in its Cutting Board and review its In and Out points. Keep enough time within the selected range for the transition, and, for an overlapping video transition or audio crossfade, leave source footage outside the selected range around the shared edit.

A two-second dissolve or wipe needs one second of source footage after the outgoing clip’s Out point and one second before the incoming clip’s In point. Each selected clip must also contain at least one second. For example, if a ten-second source is selected from seconds 0–8 and the next source is selected from seconds 2–7, the transition uses source seconds 7–9 of the first clip and seconds 1–3 of the second clip.

For these overlapping transitions, moving the outgoing Out point earlier leaves more source footage after it; moving the incoming In point later leaves more source footage before it. These adjustments also shorten the selected range, so keep enough time within both clips. Choose a shorter transition or a different source range when more footage is needed.

Fade Out/Fade In uses only the selected ranges. A two-second transition needs the final second of the outgoing clip and the first second of the incoming clip. If either selected range is too short, extend that range in the Cutting Board or choose a shorter transition.

Video transition families

  • Fade: choose Cross Dissolve to blend the two pictures, or Fade Through Color to fade the outgoing picture to Black or White at the edit and then fade in the incoming picture. Fade Through Color uses footage within the selected ranges.
  • Wipes: choose Left, Right, Up, or Down. A moving boundary reveals the incoming picture.
  • Slide: choose Left, Right, Up, or Down. Both pictures move across the frame during the change.
  • Cover: choose Left, Right, Up, or Down. The incoming picture moves over the outgoing picture.
  • Reveal: choose Left, Right, Up, or Down. The outgoing picture moves away to expose the incoming picture.
  • Circle: choose Open or Close. A circular area expands or contracts during the change.
  • Pixel Dissolve: individual pixels change progressively from the outgoing picture to the incoming picture.

Cross Dissolve, Wipes, Slide, Cover, Reveal, Circle, and Pixel Dissolve overlap the two sources and require footage beyond the trim points.

Choose how a transition moves

For Wipes, Slide, Cover, Reveal, and Circle, use the Ease picker after Direction or Action to change how the movement speeds up or slows down. New transitions begin with Linear.

  • Linear: moves at a constant rate throughout the transition.
  • Ease In: starts at speed, then gradually slows into the incoming clip.
  • Ease Out: starts slowly, accelerates, and finishes at speed.
  • Easy Ease: starts slowly, moves faster through the middle, then slows into the incoming clip.

Ease changes the transition movement while keeping the chosen duration and normal clip playback speed. To change an existing transition’s Ease setting, open it from Timeline Clips, choose Ease, and choose Update Transition.

Choose an audio transition

With an audio track active, move to a shared audio edit and press Command-T. Choose Crossfade or Fade Out/Fade In. Crossfade overlaps the outgoing and incoming sound and requires unused source footage. Fade Out/Fade In reaches silence at the edit and uses footage within the trims.

For Crossfade, choose a Fade Curve. Linear changes both sound levels at a steady rate. Equal Power uses a curved change to reduce the dip in loudness when blending unrelated sounds. Similar or identical sounds can become louder during an Equal Power crossfade.

For a video transition, Include Audio Transition provides independent Audio Transition Type and Audio Fade Curve choices when both clips have linked audio at the edit. Any video family can include an audio transition. Video and audio share the edit and duration, and each must have enough footage for its selected type.

Use Quick Fade

  1. Move the Editor playhead onto a clip or to a shared cut between clips.
  2. Press F to open Quick Fade in the Tool pane.
  3. Choose Fade In, Fade Out, or both. Select Fade Audio to include the linked audio.
  4. Enter each duration in seconds, then choose Apply Fade.

Use Quick Cross Dissolve or Quick Cross Fade

  1. Move the Editor playhead to an edit with an outgoing and incoming clip.
  2. Press X to open the Tool pane. A video track opens Quick Cross Dissolve. An audio track opens Quick Cross Fade.
  3. Check that the transition connects the clips you want.
  4. In a video context, choose Crossfade Audio when the clips’ audio should cross fade too.
  5. Enter the duration in seconds, then apply the transition.

Transition types

Fade In
Gradually changes the selected video from black to the clip.
Fade Out
Gradually changes the selected video from the clip to black.
Audio Fade In
Gradually raises the clip audio from silence.
Audio Fade Out
Gradually lowers the clip audio to silence.
Cross Dissolve
Fades the incoming picture over the outgoing picture across their shared edit. Crossfade Audio blends both clips’ audio at the same time.
Cross Fade
Lowers the outgoing audio while raising the incoming audio across their shared edit.
Fade Out/Fade In
Fades outgoing video through black into incoming video, or fades outgoing audio through silence into incoming audio. Half the duration fades out within the outgoing clip, reaching black or silence at the edit. The other half fades in from the incoming clip’s trimmed start. For a two-second transition at timeline second 8, the fade out runs from seconds 7–8 and the fade in from seconds 8–9.
Wipe Left
Replaces the outgoing picture with the incoming picture using an edge that moves left.
Wipe Right
Replaces the outgoing picture with the incoming picture using an edge that moves right.
Wipe Up
Replaces the outgoing picture with the incoming picture using an edge that moves upward.
Wipe Down
Replaces the outgoing picture with the incoming picture using an edge that moves downward.

Each video family can include an independently selected audio transition. Audio-only projects offer audio fades and cross fades. Each transition appears as its own Timeline clips item. Open it to edit the transition’s settings or delete it.

Trimato creates temporary render files so it can play transitions and adjusted audio. These files support the current preview and are normally removed when the preview changes or the project closes.

Change an existing transition

Open a transition from Timeline Clips to change its name, family, options, or duration in the Tool pane. For a video transition, Include Audio Transition lets you add, change, or remove its linked audio transition. Changing the duration of either part updates both parts. Update Transition saves the changes. Delete Transition asks for confirmation before removing the transition. Pressing Delete on a transition in Timeline Clips opens the same confirmation. The confirmation identifies any linked transition that will also be removed. Choose Cancel to keep the transition, or Delete Transition to remove it.
