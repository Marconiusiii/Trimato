Trimato QuickStart guide

Welcome to Trimato! Here is a quick trip from an empty project to your first fresh cut.

Start a new project

From the opening Trimato window, choose New Project. If you only want to edit and export one clip, choose Trim a Clip and jump to The Clip Editor.

For a new project:

  • Enter a project name.
  • Choose Automatic from First Clip to match the first video you add to the timeline.
  • Choose Custom to select the project resolution and frame rate yourself.
  • Turn on Use Target Duration if you want a reference length in seconds.

Choose Create Project and off you go!

Import clips

In the Project pane, choose Import Clips. Select one or more audio or video files, or select a folder, then choose Open.

Imported media appears in Project Source. Generators appear in the Generators folder. Select an imported clip and open it in the Clip Editor.

The Clip Editor

This is the bread and butter of Trimato. Or the bruschetta? Either way, this is where you shape an individual clip before adding it to your project.

The Video Clip Editor opens for video, and the Audio Clip Editor opens for audio. VoiceOver starts on the Clip playhead slider so the editing keys are ready to use. Turn Quick Nav off when using plain arrow keys.

  • Space Bar plays and pauses.
  • J plays backward, K pauses or plays, and L plays forward. Press J or L again to change the speed.
  • Left Arrow and Right Arrow move by one video frame. Audio clips move in small time steps.
  • Command+Up Arrow moves to the beginning. Command+Down Arrow moves to the end.
  • I sets the In point. O sets the Out point.
  • Command+[ removes material before the playhead. Command+] removes material after it.
  • Delete removes the section between the In and Out points.

Set an In and Out point, then press E to append that section to the project timeline. You can mark another section from the same source and press E again. Each section becomes its own timeline clip.

Other placement commands are available when you need them: W inserts and splits at the project playhead, D inserts and overwrites, and Q inserts video on top.

If you opened Trim a Clip without a project, choose Export Clip instead. Press Command+W when you are ready to leave the Clip Editor.

Add a filter

Open a timeline clip in the Clip Editor and choose the Filters tab. Choose Add Filter, select an effect, then choose Add.

Set the filter controls and wait for the preview to finish. Progress appears in its own window. VoiceOver announces occasional percentage updates without taking over your navigation. Choose Cancel if you want to stop.

Review the preview, then choose Update Clip to save the filter to that timeline clip. Use Edit Filter to change an existing filter, or Remove to take it away.

Play the project

Return to the project window and move to the Editor pane. Focus the Project playhead slider to use the playback and navigation keys.

  • Space Bar plays and pauses the assembled project.
  • Command+Right Arrow moves to the next edit point.
  • Command+Left Arrow moves to the previous edit point.
  • C opens the clip at or immediately after the playhead.
  • Command+B splits the clip at the playhead.
  • F opens Quick Fade for the edit point.

At a cut between two clips, Fade Out names the clip before the playhead and Fade In names the clip after it. Enter the Fade Out Duration or Fade In Duration, then apply the fade.

Tune the timeline

The Timeline pane lists one track at a time. Choose a track with the Track picker. With VoiceOver, interact with Timeline Clips to move through that track.

On a primary track, focus a clip and press Space Bar to pick it up. Use Left Arrow or Right Arrow to choose its new position, then press Space Bar again to drop it.

On an additional track, Left Arrow and Right Arrow move the focused clip by one project frame. The clip stays on the same track.

Press Enter on a clip to reopen it in the Clip Editor. Make your changes, then press Command+U to update that timeline clip.

Press Command+I anywhere in the project to open Get Info for the focused item. The window includes details such as duration, resolution, codec, and encoder when they are available. Press Command+W to close it.

Add something from scratch

Press Command+G in the Editor to open Generator. Choose Black, Solid Color, Static Gradient, Silence, or Text. Set its duration, choose where it should go, and add it to the timeline. The new item appears in the Generators folder in Project Source.

Export your project

Clear both project In and Out points to export the complete timeline. Set both points if you only want part of it.

Press Command+E or choose File > Export Project. Enter a filename, choose a location and format, then choose Export.

Export progress appears in its own window. You can keep track of the percentage or choose Cancel Export. When the export finishes, Trimato sends a notification with the filename if notifications are allowed.

And there it is: your first fresh cut from Trimato!
